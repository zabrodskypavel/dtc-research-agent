## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Native Pet

*(First pet-supplement brand to anchor Section 2 in this brief's run — every prior spotlight has been a human-supplement brand.)*

**Native Pet**, a St. Louis clean-label pet-supplement brand founded in 2017 by childhood friends Dan Schaefer (CEO) and Pat Barron, ranked **No. 890 on the 2026 Inc. 5000** with **390.7% three-year revenue growth**. Its flagship product, The Daily (a multi-benefit chew for mobility, energy, gut, and skin/coat), earned **Clean Label Project certification in 2025**, verified against 150+ contaminants — a credibility signal the brand leans on heavily against a crowded, largely unregulated pet-supplement category.

**Why it's grown:** A "digital-first, retail-second" trajectory — Native Pet built its brand and customer base online before deliberately entering physical retail starting in early 2024 (Tractor Supply), then broadening to PetSmart, Target, CVS, and specialty/natural grocery. That sequencing (prove DTC demand, then use it as leverage into big-box) is the core growth mechanic, and the brand is now in **13,000+ retail locations** nationwide alongside Amazon, Chewy, and Thrive Market.

**Acquisition channels and tactics:** A mix of brand and performance marketing to build the initial DTC following (the company explicitly frames its strategy as "brand and performance marketing" working together), plus referral programs that lean on the tight-knit, high-trust nature of pet-owner communities — pet owners referring other pet owners converts unusually well versus generic paid acquisition.

**Retention/loyalty tactics:** Subscription-first purchasing tied to natural 30/60/90-day product consumption cycles; a rewards program layered on top, though public detail on its specific tier structure is thin.

**Primary marketing channels:** Digital/social-first brand-building historically, now supplemented by retail merchandising and in-store presence as the newer growth lever. The company hired its first-ever Chief Marketing Officer (Ariel Stoddard) recently, described internally as a deliberate step from "category challenger" to "fully scaled consumer brand."

**Geographic markets:** US-only; no international retail or DTC expansion found.

**What makes the business model unique:** Third-party contamination testing (Clean Label Project certification) as a trust wedge in a supplement category that skeptics regularly flag for weak substantiation — paired with a proven "DTC-proof-then-retail-scale" playbook that mirrors what several human-supplement brands in this brief (Cymbiotika, Bloom) have also used, but applied earlier and more aggressively in pet.

**Balance note:** Total funding is modest (~$30.5M, including an $11M Series B led by CAVU Consumer Partners, with Mars Inc. among 12 investors — notable as a strategic/industry investor). Reviews are mixed: recurring complaints on customer-service responsiveness, order fulfillment, and subscription-billing friction (one cited case: a $272.99 charge for an unfulfilled order, partially refunded after two weeks), alongside genuine efficacy complaints for some SKUs (calming gummies, certain probiotics) mixed with strongly positive results reported for others (Wondergland, omega oil). No lawsuits or FTC action found.
