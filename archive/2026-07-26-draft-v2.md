## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Holy

*(Grüns and IM8 were both strong alternative picks this week — see 1a. Holy was chosen specifically because it's the most EU-native of the three growth stories in play right now.)*

Holy is a Berlin-founded (2020) DTC soft-drinks brand that's taking on Monster and Red Bull with a format built for direct-to-consumer economics rather than can-based retail: powdered energy drinks, hydration mixes, and iced teas sold in sachet or tub form, mixed at home. That format is the whole thesis — lighter to ship, cheaper to produce, and naturally suited to a subscription/DTC model, letting Holy undercut incumbents on unit economics while marketing itself as the "better-for-you" alternative.

**Why it's growing:** the brand has built a genuine European fanbase — 2.5 million-plus across Europe — and has converted that into real retail distribution: its products are now in 6,000+ stores across Germany and Austria, including major chains like Rewe and Edeka, since early 2026. A flavor/formulation partnership with Symrise, which Holy's team credits as central to "setting new standards in the powdered energy drink category," has underpinned product quality as it scales.

**Acquisition channels:** DTC-first (direct online sales, mix-at-home format) to build the fanbase, followed by a retail-led expansion into major European grocery chains — the same DTC-to-retail arc seen in most of this week's other growth stories (Arrae, Wuffes).

**Retention/loyalty tactics:** not publicly detailed in what's reachable without a login — worth a follow-up look at Holy's own channels (Instagram/TikTok, where DTC beverage brands typically run their loyalty and community plays) in a future run.

**Marketing channels:** European grocery retail placement, flavor/ingredient partnerships (Symrise) for credibility, and now a dedicated UK go-to-market team.

**Geographic markets:** Core strength in Germany and Austria; now investing **£10 million** to break into UK retail in 2026, with a London-based operations team led by Dan Macken and a former Monster Energy executive, Ben Pegram, hired to lead the push.

**What makes the model unique:** the sachet/powder format is a structural cost advantage over canned energy drinks (shipping weight, production cost) that a DTC-native brand can exploit in a way legacy can-based competitors can't easily copy without cannibalizing their own retail relationships.

**Sources:** [The Grocer](https://www.thegrocer.co.uk/news/holy-in-10m-push-to-shake-up-uk-soft-drinks-category/714673.article) (UK push) · [Tech in Berlin](https://www.techinberlin.com/news/from-powder-to-profit-how-berlin-s-holy-energy-scaled-across-europe) (growth story) · [Perfumer & Flavorist](https://www.perfumerflavorist.com/flavor/beverage/news/21877825/symrise-usa-symrise-collaborates-with-holy-energy-for-natural-energy-drink) (Symrise partnership)
