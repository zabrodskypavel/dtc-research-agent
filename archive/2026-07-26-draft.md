## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Vuori

*(Flagged: illustrative pick for this trial, not a fully verified trailing-12-month growth story — see notes below)*

Vuori is the clearest challenger brand taking direct share from Lululemon in premium athleisure, and it's doing it on a genuinely different playbook: product first, retail second, international third. The company was last valued at $5.5 billion following an $825M round led by General Atlantic, SoftBank, and Norwest Venture Partners — capital it has funneled almost entirely into physical retail and international entry rather than performance-marketing spend, a reversal of the DTC playbook that built brands like it a decade ago.

**Why it's growing:** Vuori built its identity around "gym-to-street" versatility rather than a single category (yoga, running, golf) the way most challenger activewear brands do, which widens the addressable customer beyond core athletes. Category watchers consistently name it alongside Alo Yoga as the two brands actually taking wallet share from Lululemon's core customer, rather than just growing the overall pie.

**Acquisition channels:** Retail is now the primary growth lever, not paid social — the brand is targeting 100+ stores globally by the end of 2026, with its first international doors opening in Seoul and Beijing. Stores function as the acquisition channel in new markets, ahead of any digital spend.

**Retention & loyalty:** Public detail here is thin — the brand does not talk publicly about a formal loyalty program, leaning instead on product repeat-purchase (multiple fits/colors per franchise) as its retention engine.

**Marketing channels:** Store openings, product drops, and founder/athlete storytelling rather than heavy performance marketing — consistent with a brand that no longer needs to buy its way into consideration.

**Geographic markets:** Core US, expanding into South Korea and China in 2026, with a stated goal of 100+ international stores.

**What makes the model unique:** Menswear-first heritage (rare among challenger activewear DTC brands, most of which start in womenswear) combined with a retail-led international expansion funded by growth equity rather than ad spend — a notably different capital strategy than the Meta-ad-fueled DTC brands of 2016–2021.

**Sources:** [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/12/19/how-vuori-is-taking-on-lululemon.html) (valuation/funding, Dec 2024) · [Yahoo Finance / Reuters](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/vuori-plots-international-growth-15-130800811.html) (international store plans)
