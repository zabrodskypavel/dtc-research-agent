## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Tonic Health

Tonic Health, a UK vitamin-and-supplement brand founded in 2019/2020 by Sunna van Kampen, has built one of the UK's fastest-growing nutrition businesses on an almost entirely organic-social foundation, and 2026 has been its year of retail breakout.

**Why it's grown.** Van Kampen's own social content — credited with roughly 390M organic views in 2024 and, per the brand's own fundraising materials, 1.6M followers generating 30M+ organic views monthly — built Tonic's audience before most of its retail distribution existed. Revenue (self-reported via a Republic crowdfunding filing, not independently audited) is cited at a ~£9.5M run-rate with 2025 revenue up 129% YoY; trade press separately described it as the UK's fastest-growing vitamin brand in an October 2026 headline. Treat the precise revenue figures as company-sourced, not audited.

**Acquisition channels/tactics.** Overwhelmingly organic social-first (TikTok/Instagram), rather than paid-performance-driven — unusual among brands this brief covers. A standout tactic: an August 2026 licensing partnership with Mattel, putting limited-edition Barbie and Hot Wheels packaging (collectible glass jars/tubs) on its bestselling Kids Multi Gummies, plus a Thomas & Friends edition of its Baby Vitamin D3 Drops — sold DTC, on Amazon, and through retail partners at standard pricing while stocks last.

**Retention/loyalty.** Not well-documented publicly; the brand reads as retail/DTC-hybrid rather than subscription-first.

**Marketing channels.** Founder-led organic social content is the primary engine; retail shelf placement and licensed-IP collabs are secondary.

**Geography.** UK-concentrated — nationwide across Tesco, Sainsbury's, Asda, Morrisons, Waitrose, Ocado, Boots, and Superdrug, plus a nationwide Holland & Barrett kids-gummies launch. One lower-confidence source describes a Walmart (US) partnership; not independently corroborated this run.

**What makes the model unique.** A nutrition brand built social-audience-first, monetized later through retail rather than DTC-subscription economics, and now using toy/character licensing (Mattel) as a kids'-vitamin differentiation play rather than typical influencer-seeding.

**Balance note.** Trustpilot ~4.5/5 (UK, ~594 reviews), mostly positive; isolated subscription-cancellation complaints, nothing systemic found. No lawsuit or FTC action found. One item worth flagging for context, not as a red flag: van Kampen took on a paid strategic-advisor role (equity vesting over two years) at a separate ready-meal brand, Hide, in mid-2026 — reported as an additional role, not a departure from Tonic.

**Runner-ups considered:** Apothékary (targeting ~$40M 2026 revenue, 30-40% YoY growth per company claims — solid but the dollar figures are self-reported and inconsistent across sources); Perelel (claims revenue "more than doubled" annually since 2020, but no 2026 figure exists beyond a November 2025 funding round).
