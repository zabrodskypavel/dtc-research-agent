## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Bundle x Joy

**Bundle x Joy**, a Phoenix-based pet gut-health brand founded in 2021 by Jess Berger (ex-PetSmart/Nestlé Purina merchandiser, after her own Great Dane's unresolved digestive issues), is this week's strongest *fresh, verifiable* growth story in the supplement space — with a genuine caveat: its product line spans gut-health powders/toppers (the closest thing to a pure supplement, its "Daily Boost" bone-broth/colostrum powder) alongside jerky toppers and allergy-friendly kibble, so it reads as food-and-treats-adjacent rather than a pure pill/capsule supplement brand. It's flagged here rather than silently normalized, because it's genuinely the freshest and most verifiable candidate this cycle (see runner-ups below).

**Why it's grown:** Ranked **No. 170 on the 2026 Inc. 5000 list** (published Aug 11, 2026), posting **2,300% revenue growth from 2022–2025** and reaching seven-figure revenue in year one — the freshest dateable catalyst evaluated this week (within the last two months), layered on a steady 2025 retail cadence: Whole Foods/Sprouts debut, Walmart nationwide (Apr 2025, billed as "first Latina-founded pet brand" on Walmart shelves), and a national PetSmart launch (Oct 2025, 500+ stores). Now in **1,400+ retail doors** total, with confirmed listings at Costco, Chewy, and Thrive Market.

**Acquisition channels/tactics:** Early traction via Amazon's seller program (seven-figure year-one sales) and DTC; growth now leans on retail-as-acquisition — physical shelf placement at mass/specialty/club retailers — over a visible paid-performance engine. PR-heavy earned media (Inc. 5000, NEXTY Award finalist at Expo West, a FedEx small-business feature) substitutes for large-scale paid social.

**Retention/loyalty:** No major subscription case study found; messaging emphasizes repeat purchase and word-of-mouth, with the consumable/topper format naturally driving reorder cadence.

**Marketing channels:** Earned/trade press, founder-story press (the Latina-founder, first-gen-American narrative), and retailer co-marketing at launch events — no evidence of a large paid-social or influencer program, consistent with a smaller marketing budget relative to its retail footprint.

**Geography:** US-only; no international expansion found.

**Business model:** A food/treats/supplement "bundle" rather than a single-SKU pill play — gut-health powders, jerky, toppers, and accessories sold as one system — with 3% of every purchase donated to women entrepreneurs and animal-welfare causes. Backed by a notably capital-efficient $1M seed (Mars Petcare Ventures, Leap Venture Studio, R/GA Ventures, Michelson Found Animals Foundation) relative to the scale of its retail footprint.

**Balance note:** No lawsuits, FTC actions, or notable negative-review pattern found; scattered complaints cite price vs. mass dog food and some picky-eater pushback — minor, not systemic.
