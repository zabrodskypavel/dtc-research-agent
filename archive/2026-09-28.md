## Section 2 — Brand Spotlight: Nutrafol

**Nutrafol**, the #1 dermatologist-recommended hair-growth supplement brand (majority-owned by Unilever since 2022), has crossed **$1 billion in annual sales** and is now valued at roughly **$3.5 billion**, per a March 23, 2026 Forbes exclusive with CEO Cindy Gustafson — the freshest and largest-scale growth story evaluated this week, ahead of runner-up **Perelel** (women's health/prenatal brand, $27M growth round from Prelude Growth Partners, Nov 2025 — smaller scale, and its "doubled revenue YoY" claim is self-reported without an independently verified figure) and **PetLab Co.** (rejected — its revenue estimates rely on low-confidence aggregator data rather than disclosed or trade-press-verified figures, and it carries a heavier pattern of subscription-related complaints).

**Why it's grown:** Three concrete moves over the trailing year. In October 2025, Nutrafol became the first hair-growth supplement to earn **NSF Certified for Sport** status and signed a multi-year partnership with **Major League Baseball** as its official hair-growth partner (launched via the "Hats Off" postseason campaign, continuing into the 2026 season as "The Long Game"). It has also broadened retail distribution into Ulta Beauty, while continuing to dominate the physician/dermatology channel — its core differentiator. Online-store sales alone were roughly $95M in 2025 (up double digits YoY per third-party e-commerce trackers), a small fraction of total revenue, which is now majority direct-to-physician and omnichannel retail rather than pure DTC.

**Acquisition channels and tactics:** A network of 3,000+ dermatologists, plastic surgeons, and stylists who recommend and directly dispense the product — patients get "physician pricing" through dermatology offices — supplemented by broad influencer/creator marketing and now a sports-sponsorship halo via the MLB deal.

**Retention/loyalty tactics:** A subscription structure built around a 6-bottle auto-renew commitment, reinforced by the **myNutrafol app** (progress photos, naturopathic-doctor consults, a bundled Headspace subscription, and a "Real Growth Guarantee").

**Primary marketing channels:** Physician-channel dispensing as the primary sales/trust mechanism (unusual for a supplement brand), plus influencer marketing and sports sponsorship.

**Geographic markets:** Still overwhelmingly US-focused. Gustafson told Forbes international expansion will be "exceptionally methodical," seeded through physician networks, with the first international doctor's-office placement targeted by end of 2026.

**What makes the business model unique:** Treating dermatologists as a primary sales channel rather than pure DTC — a trust/distribution wedge most supplement brands don't have — combined with a hardware/software retention layer (the app) that's unusually sophisticated for the category.

**Balance note:** The growth comes with real friction. Consumer watchdog TINA.org filed a complaint with the FTC and FDA over Nutrafol's "clinically proven" claims and undisclosed influencer relationships (30+ flagged ads), which fed a subsequent class-action lawsuit alleging deceptive marketing. Trustpilot reviews are mixed, with recurring complaints about price (~$88/month) and difficulty cancelling via chat-only support.
